### The ZoomInfo cautionary tale (directly relevant: Deepline plans a side panel)

ZoomInfo's July 2026 rebuild moved the extension from a floating overlay into Chrome's native side panel. ZoomInfo's developer reply says it did this "to improve speed." The rating then collapsed to 1.6 (295 ratings, 300K users). The reviews from 2026-07-15 to 2026-09-02 cite:

- **Lost features:** "removed about 95% of the functionality"; missing HQ address and employee count; the old expandable mini-site overlay is gone.
- **Side-panel conflict:** "opening the side panel closes another side panel they use constantly." Chrome shows one side panel at a time, so a panel competes with Gemini, Claude, Apollo, and others. Also: "shrinks the webpage on smaller screens."
- **Auth:** "login fails even while logged into Zoominfo in another tab. Clicking login just refreshes the page."
- **Stability:** "stops working and must be shut down after every contact export."
- **"Restore the previous version"** shows up in many reviews.

Surfe saw the same arc in September 2026: "Right now it's big, you need to click a lot, and it takes much of memory" (2026-09-21), and "made big changes... poor, unusable, slow, and lost all that made it good" (2026-09-09). Salesloft Connect sits at 1.2.

**Lessons for Deepline:**

1. **Auth must reuse the web-app session.** Use the deepline.com cookie through `chrome.cookies`, or a token minted by the logged-in tab. Never ship a separate login that can desync.
2. **Treat the side panel as shared real estate.** Keep it narrow, useful at about 320px, and quick to dismiss. Consider a tiny fallback: popup or badge-only mode, so users of other panels aren't forced to choose.
3. **Never ship a redesign that removes a used feature.** Feature-flag new UI server-side and keep parity.
4. **Each action must leave the panel in a usable state** (no "restart after export").
5. **One click to a result is the whole review-score game.**

## TL;DR

- **"Frontier" = befrontier.com (formerly Exportly, exportly.ai).** A rep-facing Chrome extension that acts as the "execution layer" for plays RevOps builds in Clay. Reps click on a LinkedIn profile, company site, or CRM record. Frontier runs Clay's waterfall with the team's own Clay credits, shows Claygent talking points, and syncs to the CRM or fires the record into a Clay table that triggers an ops-built play. It also has an "AI Mode" chat that scans a territory. Reported $13M from NFX and Firstminute. Enterprise-only pricing. No public CWS listing was found (distribution looks private or enterprise). This is almost exactly the Deepline pitch with "Clay table" swapped for "Deepline play webhook."
- **The market splits into three shapes:**
  1. **Data-vendor overlays** (Apollo, Lusha, ContactOut, Kaspr, Seamless, RocketReach, Cognism). They inject a widget into LinkedIn and reveal email/phone from their own DB for credits.
  2. **CRM bridges** (Surfe, HubSpot Sales, lemlist, Common Room, Unify, Crunchbase). They show "is this person in my CRM, add/enrich/sequence."
  3. **Workflow launchers** (Frontier, Clay for Chrome, Supered Prospector). The extension is a thin trigger into server-side workflows. Deepline belongs in group 3.
- **The LinkedIn risk is real and growing.** LinkedIn's JS probes for more than 6,000 extensions through `chrome-extension://<id>/<resource>` fetches (BleepingComputer, April 2026). It explicitly targets more than 200 sales tools, including Apollo, Lusha, and ZoomInfo. LinkedIn removed the Apollo and Seamless.AI company pages on 2025-03-06 (Apollo was later restored). LinkedIn's help center bans extensions that "scrape, modify the appearance of, or automate activity on" LinkedIn. **Design implication:** no DOM injection on linkedin.com, no web_accessible_resources, read only the URL (plus at most minimal visible text on an explicit click), and do every lookup server-side.

## Speed and weight observations

- Package size is a decent proxy for client-side complexity: Crunchbase 54 KiB, RocketReach 325 KiB, Lusha 770 KiB, ContactOut 857 KiB, Seamless 995 KiB, Clay 3.8 MiB, lemlist 4.2 MiB, Surfe 4.2 MiB, Apollo 11.4 MiB, Common Room 15.6 MiB, Kaspr 22 MiB, HubSpot 64.9 MiB.
- The only sustained performance complaints found were HubSpot Sales (Gmail CPU/RAM) and Surfe ("LinkedIn a bit slower"). Both inject persistent UI into heavy SPAs. Side panels run in their own document and do not tax the host page.

## LinkedIn crackdown (2024-2026)

- **Extension probing:** LinkedIn serves a randomized-name JS file that fetches `chrome-extension://<id>/<web_accessible_resource>` for about 6,236 extension IDs. It also collects a device fingerprint (cores, memory, screen, timezone, battery, audio). It checked about 2,000 IDs in 2025 and more than 6,000 by April 2026. More than 200 targeted IDs are sales tools (Apollo, Lusha, ZoomInfo). LinkedIn says it uses the data "to determine which extensions violate its terms" and to explain accounts "fetching an inordinate amount of other members' data." [BleepingComputer, 2026-04-03]
  - **Mitigation:** probing only works if the extension exposes `web_accessible_resources`. Ship **none**. If one is unavoidable, set `use_dynamic_url: true` (Chrome 130+), which randomizes the per-session URL. Timing side channels still exist. [Chromium PSA]
- **Policy:** LinkedIn prohibits "browser plug-ins, or browser extensions that scrape, modify the appearance of, or automate activity on LinkedIn's website", with a risk of restriction or shutdown. [LinkedIn help a1341387]
- **Enforcement:** on 2025-03-06 LinkedIn removed the Apollo.io and Seamless.AI company pages (Apollo was restored; Apollo then dropped LinkedIn prospecting messaging). Common Room removed post-engagement capture in December 2024. Kaspr added a hide-on-LinkedIn toggle and publishes visit-rate guidance. Anecdotal profile-view thresholds are around 80-100 per day on free accounts and 150-200 on Sales Nav (Indie Hackers, unverified).
- **No source found** that confirms a user restriction triggered *solely* by having Apollo installed. Restrictions correlate with volume and automation, and the probing gives LinkedIn the attribution.

## Chrome platform notes

- **Side panel API** (Chrome 114+, MV3): `"permissions": ["sidePanel"]`, `side_panel.default_path`. Call `sidePanel.setPanelBehavior({openPanelOnActionClick:true})` in the service worker. Use `setOptions({tabId, path, enabled})` for per-tab panels that hide on irrelevant tabs. `open()` (116+) requires a user gesture. `close()` 141+, `onOpened` 141+, `onClosed` 142+, `getLayout()` 140+. The panel path must be local to the package. [developer.chrome.com sidePanel]
- **CWS MV3 policy:** no remotely hosted logic. Server calls for data, config, and feature flags are fine as long as all logic ships in the package. Code in isolated iframes is exempt from the remote-code rule but is still judged under the data and Limited Use rules. Reviewers reject or remove extensions whose full functionality they can't determine. Single purpose and Limited Use apply, so declare PII handling the way every listing above does. [CWS MV3 requirements]
- **Implication:** a server-driven "play list" (names, input schemas) is data and is fine. Downloading JS per play is not.

## What to steal

1. **Frontier's split:** ops (or the agent) authors plays; the rep sees a short list of named buttons for the current page. Deepline maps this 1:1: button = POST to the play webhook with `{url, page_type, selection}`.
2. **Context detection from the URL only:** `linkedin.com/in/<slug>`, `/company/<slug>`, `/sales/lead/...`, a bare domain, a Gmail thread sender, a SFDC/HubSpot record id. Then resolve server-side. Crunchbase (54 KiB) and Clay's "send the URL only" approach show this is enough.
3. **CRM-presence badge first** (Frontier, Surfe, Unify, Common Room): "already in HubSpot / owned by X" before any spend, to avoid dupes and wasted credits.
4. **Cost shown before running, result streamed after:** show the play's credit estimate on the button and stream run status into the panel. This answers the "credits burned" complaint aimed at Apollo and Kaspr.
5. **Accept / edit / dismiss cards** for AI output (Amplemarket Duo).
6. **Recipes** (Clay): map a page shape once, reuse it on similar pages. Later; skip for v1.
7. **Admin guardrails** (Frontier): site allowlist, per-play permissions, usage caps, run logs. These probably already exist in Deepline as org and play settings.
8. **Opened by click, not always-on** (Common Room): the panel opens from the toolbar icon or a keyboard shortcut.

## What to avoid

1. **Injecting UI into linkedin.com.** That is the explicit policy violation and the thing that makes HubSpot and Surfe feel slow. Use the native side panel only.
2. **Any `web_accessible_resources`.** That is the probe surface. Ship none (or use `use_dynamic_url`).
3. **Content scripts that read LinkedIn's DOM or Voyager API in the background, or auto-scrape lists.** If page text is needed, read it on demand via `activeTab` plus `scripting.executeScript` *after* the user clicks, and send it to the server. Never paginate or auto-visit.
4. **Heavy bundles** (HubSpot 65 MiB, Kaspr 22 MiB). Target under 200 KiB with a plain TS/Preact panel.
5. **Hard dependency on a third-party engine** (Frontier on Clay). Deepline owns the play runtime, which is the advantage.
6. **Opaque credit spend** (Apollo's credit cuts, Kaspr's billing complaints). Always show the cost and the remaining balance.
7. **Stale builds** (Clay v1.0.0 from April 2025, Crunchbase from October 2024). Push behavior to the server so the client rarely needs a release.
